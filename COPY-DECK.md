## 5.3 — Build and clear (the mechanism, numbered — the only numbered section)

**Heading:** Your body builds and clears all day. After fifty, one side slows down.

1. **You build small repairs constantly.** Every time a vessel takes a little wear, your body patches it. This is normal. It's supposed to happen.
2. **Then a clearing system breaks those patches back down** so the blood keeps moving freely. Build, clear, build, clear — all day, without you noticing.
3. **After about fifty, the clearing side slows.** The building side doesn't. `[CITATION NEEDED — fibrinolytic decline with age]`
4. **So you end up building like you're 25 and clearing like you're 65.** Same repairs going up. Fewer coming back down.
5. **Here's the part nobody mentions.** Diet, exercise, and almost every bottle on the counter are aimed at *building less* — less cholesterol, less plaque, less of the bad thing. Almost nothing is aimed at *clearing more*. That's the side that slowed down.

That's the whole reason this is a different conversation from your statin or your fish oil. Those work on the building side. This is about the clearing side. It supports your body's normal fibrinolytic activity — the clearing side — so blood keeps moving the way it did when the clearing kept up. `[CITATION NEEDED — nattokinase fibrinolytic mechanism]`

## 5.4 — The dose gate (most important body section)

**Heading:** The dose is the whole argument.

**Body:**
FU stands for fibrinolytic units. It's not the weight of the capsule — it's how much *clearing activity* is actually in a serving. Two bottles can hold the same milligrams of nattokinase and deliver completely different FU. FU is the number that matters. Everything else on the label is packaging.

Here's the part the ads got right: **below a real dose, the research doesn't apply to what you're holding.** The studies people quote for nattokinase were run at meaningful FU. Most bottles on the shelf sell at 2,000 to 5,000 FU. That's not a smaller version of the same thing. It's under the line where the evidence lives.

**About that one study that "found nothing."**
If you've been reading, someone has pointed you at a trial that ran for years, on a few hundred people, and found no effect — proof, they say, that nattokinase does nothing. Read the method. That trial dosed at roughly 2,000 FU. `[CITATION NEEDED — 2,000 FU RCT null result + FU used]` It didn't test a real dose and come up empty. It tested a fifth of a real dose. Nobody arguing about that study out loud has mentioned the number.

**Why most brands sit under the line.** FU is the expensive part. Raising the dose raises the cost of every single capsule, on every single bottle, forever. The cheapest way to sell "nattokinase" is to put a little in, print the milligrams, and stay quiet about the FU. `[PRODUCT FU — e.g. 10,000] FU is what it costs to be on the right side of the line.`
