Let's be honest

The hand-over-the-mouth test doesn't actually work.

You breathe into your palm. You sniff. It seems fine. So you lean in anyway — and spend the whole time half-distracted, hoping you got away with it.

Here's the uncomfortable truth: your nose literally tunes out your own breath. The one person who can't check is you.

So you stock up. Mints in one pocket, gum in the other, a bottle of mouthwash by the sink. And for about twenty minutes, you feel safe. Then it creeps back — usually at the worst possible moment.

"Why does it always come back, no matter how much I brush, rinse, and chew?"

If you've ever asked that, you're not doing anything wrong. You've just been fighting the wrong thing.

The real culprit

It was never about how clean your mouth felt.

Most bad breath isn't food and it isn't poor hygiene. It's a gas. Specific bacteria — living in the spots a toothbrush can't reach — feed and release Volatile Sulfur Compounds (VSCs). That sulfur smell is what people actually notice.

⚠ Why it always came back

You were masking the smell. Nothing was touching the source.

Cover up a sulfur factory with peppermint and you buy yourself minutes. Leave the factory running and the smell always — always — comes back. That's not bad luck. That's biology.
